Nomad Capital — Week of July 27–31, 2026

A week defined by the tension between monetary policy and U.S. data: the Fed held rates at 3.50%–3.75% amid the sharpest internal split of the year, while AI-linked tech earnings showed extreme dispersion —Microsoft added roughly $490B in market cap in a single session, an all-time record, while Meta fell about 9%—. In Argentina, Milei announced the reform of the Central Bank's Charter in a nationally televised address. The bottom line: Wall Street closes the week higher, the Merval tracks the rally, and Argentina's dollar sovereign curve shows mixed performance.

1. Timeline of the week

Day by day

Monday 7/27
Wall Street opens the week with strong risk appetite: the Dow adds more than 500 points as oil prices slide and investors rotate out of semiconductor stocks following the prior weeks' declines.
Tuesday 7/28
A cautious session ahead of the Fed decision: Treasury yields ease slightly while oil retreats and the market positions itself for Wednesday.
Wednesday 7/29
The Fed holds rates at 3.50%–3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting, with three of twelve votes favoring a hike —the most visible internal split since Warsh took over—. The Dow at one point shed nearly 800 points intraday before paring losses. The 30-year yield touches its highest level since 2007.
Thursday 7/30
A strong rally led by Microsoft and semiconductors: the Nasdaq snaps a six-session losing streak with a 2.8% jump, the S&P rises 1.7% and the Dow adds 613 points (+1.2%). Microsoft jumps 16% after reporting Azure growth accelerating to +43% year-over-year. Argentina's EMBI closes at 441 bps (−4 bps vs. Wednesday).
Friday 7/31
Wall Street closes a volatile week higher: Amazon jumps roughly 15% after AWS grew +37% year-over-year —its best pace in 18 quarters— and results comfortably beat revenue and earnings expectations, while Apple falls about 8% on weak sales guidance. The S&P 500 adds 0.7%, the Nasdaq 1% and the Dow 0.53%, even as Treasury yields keep climbing (10-year above 4.7% intraday). In Argentina, Milei announces the reform of the Central Bank's Charter in a nationally televised address: single mandate, automatic fiscal rule and a ban on Treasury financing.

3. Key drivers of the week

What moved the market

01
The Fed holds rates amid the sharpest internal split of the year
With the range at 3.50%–3.75% and three of twelve votes favoring a hike, the real 10-year rate still sat in the 99th percentile of its annual range despite second-quarter GDP coming in below expectations (1.5% vs. 2.0%) and cooler core PCE inflation. Credit markets didn't validate the tension (HY spread in the 48th percentile), suggesting a repricing around Fed credibility rather than a genuine stress event.
02
AI earnings show extreme dispersion
Microsoft added roughly $490B in market cap in a single session —the largest jump on record— after Azure growth accelerated to +43% year-over-year, while Meta fell about 9% after trimming its capex guidance and Apple slid about 8% on Friday on weak sales guidance. Amazon was the other big winner of the week: it jumped roughly 15% on Friday after AWS grew +37% year-over-year —its best pace in 18 quarters— comfortably beating revenue and earnings expectations. The market is starting to demand concrete results from each AI name individually rather than rewarding the sector as a block: whoever monetizes (Microsoft, Amazon) rallies; whoever just promises (Meta) or disappoints on guidance (Apple) gets punished hard.
03
Milei announces the reform of the Central Bank's Charter
In a nationally televised address on Thursday: a single mandate, an automatic fiscal rule with sanctions, a ban on financing the Treasury, opening of the capital markets, and insurance deregulation. Its durability will hinge on surviving a potential change in legislative majority in 2027. Separately, the gap between the blue-chip and official dollar hit its yearly high, and the Central Bank is negotiating a three-year renewal of its swap line with the People's Bank of China (~40% of gross reserves).
04
Oil holds a structural geopolitical premium
The entire curve remains in backwardation (WTI ~$14 above its 12-month contract), underpinned by the conflict in the Middle East, closed shipping routes and reduced Saudi exports. Separately, the Bank of Japan turned hawkish without moving rates, further pressuring the yen to four-decade lows despite a likely Tokyo currency intervention.
4. Signals to watch

Week of August 3–7

01
Legislative progress of the Central Bank reform
The market will start pricing in the odds of Congressional approval; its long-term durability depends on surviving a potential change in majority in 2027.
02
U.S. July employment report (payrolls)
First Friday of August: a key data point to recalibrate the odds of a September rate hike, currently near 55% after the Fed's cautious tone.
03
Evolution of the FX gap and Central Bank intervention
With the blue-chip/official gap at its yearly high, the Central Bank's response in the official and MEP markets will be the short-term thermometer for the credibility of the announced reform.
04
Continuation of the AI earnings season
With Microsoft and Amazon confirming the thesis and Meta and Apple disappointing, the market will keep rewarding concrete execution over promises —a pattern to watch in the reports still to come.
