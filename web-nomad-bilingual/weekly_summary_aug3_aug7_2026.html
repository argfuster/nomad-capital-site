Nomad Capital — Week of August 3–7, 2026

A week of sharp contrast between global and local sentiment —and where much of the global rally was driven more by narrative than by actual facts: Trump and Treasury Secretary Bessent repeated throughout the week that a deal with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would come “today or tomorrow,” sinking oil and pushing stocks higher each time they spoke —but Iran denied at every turn that it was negotiating directly with the U.S. (only with Oman), and no deal had been struck by Friday—. On top of that came a rebound in “big tech” after late July's panic and a surprisingly weak U.S. jobs report that cooled rate-hike expectations: Wall Street posted a second straight week of gains, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq hitting fresh records. Meanwhile, the joint U.S.-Japan intervention to prop up the yen began losing steam, and UWM —the largest wholesale mortgage originator in the U.S.— rattled the mortgage market with a sharp plunge. In Argentina, by contrast, the week was negative: the Merval fell ~6% and country risk climbed all week, despite the favorable global backdrop.

1. Timeline of the week

Day by day

Monday 8/3
Relief on reported signs of de-escalation in the Iran–U.S. conflict —Trump and Bessent talk up an imminent deal on the Strait of Hormuz—: stocks rise, Treasury yields fall and oil drops sharply (WTI −5.55%). Argentina's EMBI compresses sharply to 411 bps (−19 bps) and Argentine sovereign bonds close higher in Buenos Aires.
Tuesday 8/4
The positive tone continues: stocks and Treasury yields rise together, with the dollar (DXY) firmer. The Central Bank tops $49B in reserves again; YPF gains liquidity after its stock split. The EMBI rebounds to 421 bps (+10 bps).
Wednesday 8/5
Global markets rally (Nikkei +3.66%), gold kicks off a strong rise (+1.49%). INDEC confirms poverty rose again to 30% in Q1, cutting short months of improvement. The EMBI climbs to 428 bps (+7 bps) and Argentine sovereigns close mixed.
Thursday 8/6
Markets in wait-and-see mode ahead of Friday's U.S. jobs data. Argentina renews its swap with China for $19B over 5 years (through 2031) and Central Bank reserves top $50B for the first time under this administration. Despite that, country risk keeps climbing: EMBI at 446 bps (+18 bps) and GD30D falls sharply, −4.08% in Buenos Aires.
Friday 8/7
The U.S. jobs report surprises with a −23K decline (vs. +80K expected): Wall Street reads it as a sign the Fed won't hike rates soon, and stocks rally to fresh records in the S&P and Nasdaq. In Argentina, by contrast, the wholesale dollar nears $1,500 and country risk approaches the 450–460 zone, with the Central Bank buying just $41M. The IMF payment ($852M) pulls gross reserves down to $48.835B.

3. Key drivers of the week

What moved the market

01
July NFP: a sharp negative surprise, but markets celebrate it
Payrolls fell −23K vs. +80K expected —the first decline in several months—. Unemployment dropped to 4.1% (from 4.2%), but due to falling labor force participation (61.4%, a 5-year low), not real job gains: U-6 (the broad measure) held steady at 7.9%, only slightly widening the gap with U-3. Treasury yields fell (UST10 −9bps) and markets ruled out a September rate hike, fueling a record rally in stocks.
02
Big tech: from panic to euphoria
Late July's correction (Alphabet −15%, Meta −10% on AI capex fears, which cost the Mag 7 ~$797B in a single day) reversed this week: Palantir +30% on a record Q2, Intel +11%, confirming the same pattern from prior weeks —the market rewards real monetization, punishes capex promises without evidence—. The Nasdaq Composite added +5.2% for the week, its best week since April.
03
Yen: the joint U.S.-Japan intervention starts losing steam
After the first joint U.S.-Japan intervention since 2011 (Friday 7/31, with the Treasury selling euros to buy yen), the currency jumped to 155.20. But without an actual BOJ rate hike backing the announcement, the rally has already given back much of its gain: the yen is trading in the 158/dollar zone this week. Bessent (U.S. Treasury) explicitly called for the BOJ to hike rates as a necessary complement.
04
The U.S. mortgage market is rattled: UWM plunges
United Wholesale Mortgage, the largest wholesale mortgage originator in the country, plunged between 35% and 49% on Thursday after reporting a $452M net loss in Q2 and suspending its dividend for the first time since its 2021 IPO. The company received a $2.05B capital injection (Oaktree Capital + the CEO's family). The stock is down roughly 83% from its 52-week high.
05
The Central Bank reform stays at the center of the debate
With the Charter reform already announced, public debate shifted toward the institutional sustainability of the proposal —the 2010 Redrado precedent is cited as a warning that a law alone doesn't shield the Central Bank without sustained political will—. Meanwhile, country risk kept climbing all week despite the favorable global backdrop, decoupling Argentine assets from the more benign global mood.
06
Iran: markets are trading on narrative, not facts
Bessent repeatedly said during the week (Tuesday, Wednesday) that a deal with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would come “today or tomorrow,” and Trump reinforced the message, claiming “a lot of progress” had been made. Each statement sank oil and pushed stocks higher. But Iran denied at every turn that it was negotiating directly with the U.S. —only with Oman, on a separate and more limited arrangement—, and by Friday nothing had been finalized. As CNBC summarized it: “Trump teased an Iran deal that didn't come, but markets soared.” The pattern has repeated multiple times since the conflict began.
4. Signals to watch

Week of August 10–14

01
Potential new FX intervention in Japan
With the yen giving back much of its post-intervention gain, Japan has already signaled it will repeat the operation if needed. The key will be whether the BOJ backs it with more concrete rate-hike signals.
02
Legislative reaction to the Central Bank reform
The market will start pricing in the odds of Congressional approval, against a backdrop of rising country risk and the FX gap at its highs.
03
U.S. inflation data (July CPI)
With the Fed already ruling out a September hike after the weak NFP, CPI will be key to confirming whether the labor market slowdown is coming alongside contained inflation.
04
Contagion from the UWM crisis to the broader mortgage sector
The market will watch closely whether UWM's plunge is an isolated case or the first sign of broader stress in U.S. wholesale mortgage financing.
